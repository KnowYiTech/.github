# Low-Volume Hardware Is Not Small-Scale Mass Production

Low-volume hardware has its own economics.

The team may need only a handful of assemblies or a few dozen units, but the engineering burden around each build can still be high. Tooling decisions, fixture choices, test coverage, revision churn and sourcing constraints do not disappear just because the quantity is small.

This creates a structural mismatch: the order value may be modest while the decision density is high.

That is why low-volume robotics and industrial hardware can feel disproportionately slow.

The path improves when the system can distinguish between:

- decisions worth engineering once and reusing,
- one-off exceptions that should not become permanent process,
- design choices that are acceptable for prototype quantities but break at pilot scale,
- and information that should be retained because the next order will need it again.

KnowYi is designed for this environment: real hardware programs where speed, context and repeatability matter before the company is ready for mass production.

The goal is not to imitate a high-volume factory at tiny quantities. It is to make the transition from one build to the next increasingly informed.
