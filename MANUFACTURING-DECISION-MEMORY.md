# Manufacturing Decision Memory

Most manufacturing systems remember transactions.

They remember the PO, the revision, the shipment and perhaps the inspection record.

What they often fail to preserve is the reasoning that connected those events.

Why was one tolerance changed and another left alone? Why did a later revision choose a different component, finish, process or assembly approach? Which issue was predicted before production, and which only became visible after the build?

That missing reasoning is expensive because hardware programs repeat themselves imperfectly. The next build is rarely identical, but it is often similar enough that previous judgment should matter.

KnowYi calls this **manufacturing decision memory**.

The useful unit of knowledge is not just a document. It is a relationship:

**engineering condition → decision → physical result**

When those relationships accumulate, a hardware team can begin to answer better questions:

- Have we seen a similar build before?
- Which assumptions were wrong last time?
- Which decisions were validated by the finished product?
- Which exceptions should remain exceptions?
- What can be decided immediately instead of rediscovered?

That is how production history becomes more than an archive.

It becomes an operating advantage.
